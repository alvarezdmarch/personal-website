Architect and computational designer building simulation, BIM, and data-driven tools for performance-driven, resilient design.

Marcelo Álvarez holds an M.S. in Design Computation from the Georgia Institute of Technology, where his work focuses on data-driven methods for architecture and design. He also holds a professional degree in Architecture with a specialization in computational design, bringing experience from both industry and academia. His expertise spans the full spectrum of architectural project development, integrating advanced skills in design scripting, parametric modeling, custom tool development, environmental simulation workflows, and data analytics. He is particularly interested in bridging research and practice through computational tools that enhance performance-driven and innovative design processes.

He is currently a Research Engineer I/Systems Engineer I at the Georgia Tech Research Institute (GTRI), working on energy simulations for buildings and critical infrastructure.

Previously, as a Graduate Research Assistant at GTRI, he conducted research and developed advanced computational design methods integrating Building Information Modeling (BIM) and Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD). He also contributed to the development of Outdoor+, a Grasshopper plugin that leverages the urbanMicroclimateFoam solver from ETH Zurich to simulate urban microclimates. He has also served as a Teaching Assistant for the Vertically Integrated Project (VIP) Computing Building Resiliency at Georgia Tech, supporting interdisciplinary research on sustainable and occupant-centered design. His work bridges computational tool development, environmental simulation, and data-driven design for resilient built environments.
